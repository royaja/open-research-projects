Main results

This project analyses how Norges Bank communicated across its 40 most recent English-language speeches and 40 press releases, collected on 4 April 2026. The dominant communicative function was outlook and forward guidance, appearing in 36.9 percent of paragraphs — more than any other category. Institutional accountability and mandate followed at 26.6 percent, and policy explanation and justification at 20.5 percent. Risk and uncertainty (8.7 percent) and economic assessment and diagnosis (7.3 percent) were notably less prominent. In short, Norges Bank's communication in this period was oriented more toward orienting audiences to the future and explaining institutional reasoning than toward stand-alone economic diagnosis.

The topic distribution was broad, but three areas stood out. Economic activity and the labor market was the most common subject overall at 24.2 percent, closely followed by exchange rates and external conditions at 22.9 percent and interest rates and monetary stance at 21.9 percent. Financial stability and banking accounted for 18.0 percent, and inflation and prices for 13.0 percent. This matters because it shows that Norges Bank's communication was not inflation-centric: interest-rate questions were consistently framed within a wider account of labor market conditions, external developments, and the broader economic environment.

The cross-tabulation between function and topic sharpens this picture. Economic assessment and diagnosis was heavily concentrated in paragraphs about economic activity and the labor market (45.1 percent), while risk and uncertainty was most strongly linked to exchange rates and external conditions (41.5 percent). Outlook and forward guidance appeared broadly across topics, but clustered especially around economic activity and the labor market (32.9 percent) and interest rates and monetary stance (29.3 percent). These are descriptive patterns within a bounded corpus of 80 documents and should not be read as evidence of effects on audiences or the wider public debate.
